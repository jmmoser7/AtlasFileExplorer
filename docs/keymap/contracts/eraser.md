# Eraser — interaction contract

Status: shipped
Family: tool
Reference: Photoshop Eraser (Brush mode) for painted ink, Excalidraw whole-stroke delete for vector curves; extracted from `brush.md` D11 on 28 September 2026
Command: board.tool.eraser · Key: E · Palette: Eraser tool (erase, remove strokes)
Inherits: P0.*, P2.StickyInk — deviations flagged below.

The eraser's rules used to sit in the brush contract's D11. They moved here
on 28 September 2026 with their attributions. A clause marked "review rN"
was worded by the agent in that review (27 September 2026) and approved by
the user's passes r7-3..r7-7 and tx1–tx5 (28 September 2026). The keys for the two known limits are given
on those clauses: r7-6 for the 200 world units, r7-7 for the uncovered
memory-pressure case.

## Behavior matrix

D01–D17 are every tool-scoped dimension. D18–D35 are portal-only and do not apply.

| D02 | Stickiness & repeat | P2.StickyInk: the Eraser stays armed after each pass, and each pass is one undo step. | pattern | 85 |
| D03 | Gesture grammar | Left-drag erases along the pointer's path. A click erases one dab. Shift+drag or Shift+click erases a straight line from the end of the last pass, in the tab that made it; another tab starts at its press (review r11). A Shift drag takes 45° steps from its start, and Ortho (F8) never cancels them (brush D03; user pass, 28 September 2026, tr5). Painted (stamped) brush strokes are spot-erased. Vector strokes the eraser crosses are removed whole on release (user pass, 28 September 2026, tx1). Images, text, frames, and wires are never erased. | precedent | 90 |
| D04 | Click vs drag rule | As the brush (brush D04): travel past the 4 px draft threshold is a drag. A Shift press that stays within 8 screen px is a click that erases the straight line from the end of the last pass to the click point. | precedent | 90 |
| D05 | Modifiers | The Eraser shares the brush's [ ], Shift+[ ], SizeHud (size, softness, and its texture row), and Shift+right-drag for strength, and has no color wheel (brush D05). Esc closes an open HUD before it disarms. Ctrl+Z restores the last setting change. | precedent | 95 |
| D06 | Constraints & snapping | The end of a Shift pass takes only the 45° step and the Tab lock, with Ortho (F8) on or off (`painted_segment_end`, brush D06). A pass never point-snaps. | pattern | 85 |
| D07 | Direction / value locks | P2.RhinoDraft.tab: Tab during a Shift pass locks its direction; Tab again releases (brush D07). | precedent | 90 |
| D08 | Numeric / manual entry | [ ] step the eraser's width through the brush's screen-px tiers, converted through zoom. Typing digits enters nothing. | precedent | 85 |
| D09 | Preview & readouts | While the drag lasts, the vector strokes it has crossed show at 30 % opacity (user pass, 28 September 2026, tx1). During a Shift pass the eraser's band stands in until the exact cut lands (D11). | stated | 100 |
| D10 | Cursor | The tip circle is the cursor: the soft tip disc with a thin ring at the full diameter, and the OS arrow hides under it over the board (brush D10). | precedent | 90 |
| D11 | Commit | Each pass commits one journaled step: a `Patch` per painted stroke it changed, storing an erase mark (`PathData::erase`: the pass's points in the path's normalized coordinates plus the eraser tip), and a `Remove` per vector stroke it crossed. Both painters subtract the marks from the stamp (`vector_ink::apply_erase`). Within a pass coverage keeps the maximum, so a pass never erases more than its strength where it crosses itself, whatever its texture; separate passes compound. A stroke with no ink left is removed in the pass's own undo step. Ink remaining is measured on the untextured tip coverage; grain residue never keeps a stroke alive (user, 28 September 2026, r7-10). The stroke's tips, its earlier passes, and this pass are all judged as if they were Smooth. So the release reads the live preview's ink only when none of them is textured; otherwise it runs the coarse check within the frame's raster budget, or leaves it to the workers' check below. The painted result still shows the grain. No commit rasterizes a large stroke on the frame loop (user, 27 September 2026: "ran f4 erasor lock up interface on commit of comand"; user pass, 28 September 2026, tx3). Erasing never blanks strokes the eraser did not reach: while a pass lifts reached strokes out of the cache, the tiles from before the pass stand in for the rest until their own rasters land. After an erase release the un-erased stroke never paints again. The Eraser's Shift pass never stamps on the frame loop either (2026-09-27): per reached stroke, the raster workers cut the segment out of that stroke's ink, one job at a time and newest first; until the cut for the current segment lands, every move frame paints the eraser's band in its preview color over the part not yet cut, and the release takes in the cut that shows the final segment, including one that landed after the last paint (review r10). The committed erase mark is unchanged. When that cut has not landed, the release still commits the mark at once, and the preview and the band keep standing in until the cut lands; the preview then stands in for the stroke until its erased bitmap lands (review r10). A settling preview gives way only to one that shows the same strokes or newer ones: a second pass over the stroke that is released unchanged, released before its own preview exists, or ended with Esc leaves the settling preview and its band in place; a switch to another document and back finds the stroke still settling; a move of the stroke carries the preview and its band with it, and any other edit that keeps the pass applied, a vertex edit or Delete included, leaves them at their own old place until the new bitmap lands (review r14); when the workers lose the cut past the retries, the preview stands in for the stroke with the band over its uncut part (review r12). A settling preview takes its cut even while its stroke is out of view (review r12). A band stays, in view or not, until its stroke shows its raster for its content in view, the stroke's content becomes one that neither the band's pass nor a later eraser pass over the stroke committed, or its pass is undone and a new edit clears the redo; while its stroke is hidden, or its pass is undone, the band stays without painting and asks for no frames, and it paints again with the stroke's hide ghost, once the stroke is shown, or on a redo, so neither shows the un-erased stroke (review r16). Undoing a pass while its preview still settles turns the preview's band, and every later band over the same stroke, into such bands; a band whose pass is undone stays even while its stroke is out of the scene, so undoing the stroke's own Add after the pass and then redoing both brings the band back; a later eraser pass released before its preview exists, straight or freehand, adds the content it commits to the stroke's bands (review r17). Removing a stroke, by Delete or otherwise, while a band's pass is applied keeps the band without painting, hashing, or asking for frames, and a preview still settling for a stroke that leaves the scene turns into such bands; the band also ends when its tab closes or its pass is in neither the done nor the undone history, so a Ctrl+Z that brings the stroke back never shows the un-erased stroke (review r18). A band reads its stroke's content again only after the scene changed, so a band over a hidden, off-view, or undone stroke hashes nothing per frame (review r17). Frames are asked for only while this document has a cut on the workers or a band in view waits for its stroke's raster (review r14). A band counts as in view only while its stroke's own ink, padded as the board pads strokes when it culls them, meets the view (review r17). A band over a rotated stroke counts as in view only while the stroke's rotated ink box, the box around its rotated frame padded as the tiles pad its ink, meets the view, so a band over a rotated stroke out of view asks for no frames (review r19). The board paints every rotated node its paint query returns, as before review r18; that query takes the nodes whose frame lies within half the widest stroke width (200 world units) of the painted view, so ink that reaches farther past its frame, such as a wide stroke's miter spikes, blur, or caps, can still be culled at the view edge, a limit older than review r18 (known limit; user pass, 28 September 2026, r7-6). A band counts as in view only while the board paints its stroke there, so a band over a stroke that the paint query leaves out asks for no frames (review r20). A nested board's strokes never share cached bitmaps or eraser state with the host's, even under the same node id (review r12). A nested board's stroke raster that lands and is not taken within about 120 frames is dropped, and a portal still on screen asks for it again (review r16). Cached stroke bitmaps and eraser stand-ins belong to one document and one nesting: a stroke with the same node id in another tab, or in another portal of the same nested board, never paints them (review r14). Closing a tab frees at once its settles, eraser line jobs, ink checks, queued stroke raster jobs, landed stroke rasters, and every cached bitmap of its strokes, nested boards' and removed strokes' included; a raster a worker is still building for it is dropped when it lands (review r16). An eraser preview standing in paints only while the pass that made it is still applied in its document's undo history: after an undo of that pass, the stroke paints the bitmap it had before the pass, where one is cached, until its restored bitmap lands (review r12); a redo paints a cached bitmap that shows the pass again, the erased preview or the erased bitmap that replaced it, and a vertex edit, a Delete of picked vertices, or a move keeps the pass applied (review r14). The redo holds also when the restored stroke's bitmap was rebuilt in between, at another zoom or after an earlier undo: once an eraser stand-in starts a stroke's chain, each new bitmap keeps up to two bitmaps of other contents behind it, and a late raster of other content never replaces a bitmap of the stroke's current content; the memory budget for stroke bitmaps can still evict a stroke's whole chain, and a redo after that is not covered (review r16; known limit, user pass, 28 September 2026, r7-7). The release stamps the segment on the frame loop only when the segment's box fits the frame's `SYNC_STAMP_PX` budget, or after the workers lost that cut past the retries (review r10). A stroke the pass crossed before its preview was built (a flick) keeps painting its cached tiles under the band until its erased bitmap is current; the release checks that stroke's coarse erase on the frame loop only within the same budget, and past it commits the mark at once (review r10). Whenever the release cannot tell within that budget whether a pass left a stroke any ink, straight or freehand, the raster workers run the same check, on the untextured coverage (r7-10); a stroke with no ink left is then removed in the pass's own undo step, so one undo restores the stroke with its ink and one redo removes it again (review r11). A later eraser pass that commits before the answer is taken in takes over the check of a stroke still as the earlier pass committed it: the stroke leaves in the later pass's undo step instead, so undoing that pass brings it back as the earlier pass left it, fully erased, and undoing the earlier pass restores its ink (review r13). Otherwise, if an undo, another edit, or a switch to another document comes before the answer is taken in (answers are taken in only between gestures), nothing is removed: the stroke stays, fully erased, and its erased pixels do not pick (review r11; reworded review r13). In an image paint session the release decides the active layer's marks by the coarse check within the same budget, since layer marks have no live preview; past it the mark commits at once and stays, even when fully erased, since the workers' check covers board strokes only (review r12). The layer composite that then re-stamps the erased mark still builds on the frame loop; moving it to the raster workers is open (review r14). | stated | 100 |

## Feel constants

- `SYNC_STAMP_PX`: 256 × 256 stamp pixels on the frame loop per frame (brush D11)
- Live erase previews built per frame: 3
- Touched vector strokes during a pass: 30 % opacity

## Golden paths

- **GP1:** Paint a smooth stroke, arm the Eraser with Pencil in its style row, and erase the whole stroke in one freehand pass. The release removes the stroke in the pass's undo step; one Ctrl+Z restores it (`a_full_pencil_eraser_pass_removes_the_stroke`).
- **GP2:** The same with the user's Pencil eraser over a stroke too big for the frame's raster budget: the workers' check removes it in the pass's undo step, nothing stamped on the frame loop (`a_deferred_pencil_eraser_pass_that_empties_a_big_stroke_removes_it`).
- **GP3:** A pass across a vector curve shows it at 30 % while the drag lasts; the release removes it whole; one Ctrl+Z restores it (`the_eraser_dims_crossed_curves_and_removes_them_whole`).
