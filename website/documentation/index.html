THE FIELD GUIDE

A little orientation.

From your first workbook to a presentation worth sharing. Here are the essentials for finding your way around Slate.

01 / INSTALLATION

A home on your desktop.

Download the stable Windows installer, run it, and open Slate from Start or its desktop shortcut. The installer includes both Slate and File Atlas; Windows x64 is the supported platform.

You do not need a developer toolchain. Setup installs the WebView2 and Visual C++ runtimes if they are missing, which may require an internet connection.

Core viewing, editing and export work locally without an account. Optional AI and external application integrations have their own requirements.

02 / YOUR FIRST WORKBOOK

Start with a blank canvas.

Choose New from Home to begin a workbook. Bring in images and files, add text or shapes, and arrange them on the Board. Save your work as a .slate file.

A workbook holds your layout and links to your files. Use frames to gather a composition or mark out a slide. You can keep several workbooks open in tabs and return to saved work from Home.

For the current tools and bindings, open Advanced → Commands & shortcuts inside Slate. The command reference follows the app as it evolves.

03 / WORKING WITH FILES

Your files stay yours.

Slate links to source files rather than copying them into the workbook. Keep those sources available at their original locations so their links continue to work.

Place files alongside text and drawings to build a visual working space. Workbook tags help describe and organize linked material. File Atlas, included with the installer, provides a separate visual way to explore folders.

Saving a .slate workbook saves its document and file links. When handing work to someone else, consider which source files they will need as well.

04 / PRESENTING & SHARING

Make room for the story.

Frames on the Board become slides. Compose your content inside them, set the deck sequence, and use presentation mode to walk through your work.

Export an HTML presentation for viewing in a browser. Keep the editable .slate workbook when you want to return to the canvas and keep developing the work.

05 / STAYING UP TO DATE

A workspace that keeps growing.

Installed copies check for updates in the background when opened. You can also check from Advanced → Software updates. An offline check will not stop you opening your documents.

When an update is ready, save your workbooks and close other Slate and File Atlas windows before choosing Update and restart. Your saved workbooks, settings and cache stay in their existing locations.

Stable releases are the usual starting point. Preview builds offer newer work in a separate installation and may change more often.

06 / GIVING FEEDBACK

Help shape what comes next.

Use the Suggestion box to share an idea or report a problem on GitHub. Describe what you were trying to do, what happened, and what would have helped.

Inside Slate, Advanced → Suggestion box can save a local report with app details, screenshots and optional recorded steps. These reports stay on your machine until you share them. Attach the relevant files to your GitHub issue when they help explain the problem.
